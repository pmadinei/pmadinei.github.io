01 About

I'm a PhD candidate in Psychological & Brain Sciences at UC Santa Barbara, advised by Dr. Miguel Eckstein in the Vision & Image Understanding Lab. My work sits at the intersection of computer vision, vision-language models, and human visual perception.

I'm interested in a two-way exchange between minds and models: using what we know about eye movements, gaze, and foveated perception to build more efficient and more aligned multimodal AI, and using those models, in turn, as instruments for understanding how people see. Recently that's meant layer pruning and low-rank compression for VLMs, training-free gaze-driven disambiguation for visual question answering, and measuring where human and model scene understanding diverge.

I earned my M.S. in Computer Science from UCSB, and my dual B.S. in Computer Science and Biology from the University of Tehran. Outside the lab, you'll usually find me on a tennis court or, in winter, snowboarding.

03 Research

Where minds and models look

Gaze

Gaze & foveated perception for AI

Using human eye-tracking and saccades as a signal for models: where people look tells a model where to compute and what a question is really about.

Efficiency

Efficient vision-language models

Making large multimodal models cheaper to run through interleaved layer pruning, low-rank compression, and low-rank attention, without giving up accuracy.

Alignment

The human–VLM scene understanding gap

Counterfactual Semantic Saliency scores an object's importance by the semantic shift its removal causes. Against a large human baseline, VLMs over-rely on large, central, salient objects and under-weight people.

Search

Active visual search

Language-guided, foveated models that move a simulated fovea to find targets, modeling visual search the way the visual system actually does it.

NeurIPS 2026Conference

Revealing the Gap in Human and VLM Scene Description through Counterfactual Semantic Saliency

Ziqi Wen, Parsa Madinei, Miguel P. Eckstein

CSS is a black-box, model-agnostic framework that scores an object's importance by the semantic shift caused when it is causally ablated from a scene. Tested against a human psychophysics baseline of 16,603 responses over 307 natural scenes and 1,306 counterfactual variants, VLMs over-rely on object size, centrality, and low-level saliency, and are less sensitive than people to the presence of humans.

Activation-Informed Pareto-Guided Low-Rank Compression for Efficient LLM/VLM

Ryan Solgi, Parsa Madinei, Jiayi Tian, Rupak Swaminathan, Jing Liu, Nathan Susanj, Zheng Zhang

PGSVD is a zero-shot compression pipeline that bounds the change in network loss via layer-wise activation-based errors and casts low-rank compression as a bi-objective problem, where a single uniform tolerance yields Pareto-optimal heterogeneous ranks. Better accuracy at the same compression level, with inference speedup, on both LLMs and VLMs.

CVPR 2026 · FindingsConference · first author

INTERLACE: Interleaved Layer Pruning and Efficient Adaptation in Large Vision-Language Models

Parsa Madinei, Ryan Solgi, Ziqi Wen, Jonathan Skaza, Miguel P. Eckstein, Ramtin Pedarsani

A finetune-freeze framework that analyzes triplets of consecutive layers: prune the most redundant of the first two, finetune the survivor, and freeze the third as a stable anchor. Retains 88.9% of average performance after dropping 25% of the network, finetuning on just 1% of FineVision for one epoch.

arXiv 2026Preprint

Tucker Bottleneck Attention for Multi-Dimensional Sequence Modeling

Ryan Solgi, Parsa Madinei, Zheng Zhang

TuBA projects hidden tensors into compact Tucker cores, runs self-attention on the cores, and writes the update back to the ambient space for subquadratic global token mixing. Versus standard self-attention it cuts error and computation by up to 24.7% and 66.6% on video prediction and 37.1% and 85.1% on autoregressive weather forecasting, with speedups up to 4.27×.

arXiv 2026Preprint · first author

IRIS: Intent Resolution via Inference-time Saccades for Open-Ended VQA in Large Vision-Language Models

Parsa Madinei*, Srijita Karmakar*, Russell A. Cohen Hoffing, Felix Gervitz, Miguel P. Eckstein (* equal contribution)

A training-free method that uses real-time eye tracking to resolve ambiguous questions. Fixations closest to speech onset are the most informative, more than doubling accuracy on ambiguous VQA (35.2% → 77.2%) while preserving performance on unambiguous queries. Comes with a new gaze + speech + image benchmark and a real-time interactive protocol.

DReX: Pure Vision Fusion of Self-Supervised and Convolutional Representations for Image Complexity Prediction

Jonathan Skaza, Parsa Madinei, Ziqi Wen, Miguel P. Eckstein

A vision-only model that fuses multi-scale ResNet-50 features with DINOv3 ViT-S/16 representations through learnable attention to predict how complex people find an image. State of the art on IC9600 (Pearson r = 0.9581) with roughly 21.5× fewer learnable parameters than prior multimodal approaches.
