About

I'm a PhD candidate in Psychological & Brain Sciences at UC Santa Barbara, advised by Dr. Miguel Eckstein in the Vision & Image Understanding Lab. My work sits at the intersection of computer vision, vision-language models, and human visual perception.

I'm interested in a two-way exchange between minds and models: using what we know about eye movements, gaze, and foveated perception to build more efficient and more aligned multimodal AI — and using those models, in turn, as instruments for understanding how people see. Recently that's meant work on layer pruning and low-rank compression for VLMs, training-free gaze-driven disambiguation for visual question answering, and reinforcement learning for foveated active visual search.

I earned my M.S. in Computer Science from UCSB, and my dual B.S. in Computer Science and Biology from the University of Tehran. Outside the lab, you'll usually find me on a tennis court or, in winter, snowboarding.

Research

What I work on

Efficiency

Efficient vision-language models

Making large multimodal models cheaper to run through interleaved layer pruning, low-rank compression, and visual-token reduction — without giving up accuracy.

Gaze

Gaze & foveated perception for AI

Using human eye-tracking and saccades as a supervisory signal: where people look tells a model where to compute and what a question is really about.

Search

Active visual search

Reinforcement-learning agents with a frozen VLM backbone that move a foveated sensor to find targets, modeling search the way the visual system actually does it.

Perception

The human–VLM scene perception gap

Counterfactual Semantic Saliency (CSS) scores an object's importance by the semantic shift its removal causes. Measured against a large human baseline, it reveals a scene-perception gap: VLMs over-rely on large, central objects while under-weighting people.

INTERLACE: Interleaved Layer Pruning and Efficient Adaptation in Large Vision-Language Models

Parsa Madinei, Ryan Solgi, Ziqi Wen, Jonathan Skaza, Miguel P. Eckstein, Ramtin Pedarsani

A finetune-freeze framework that analyzes triplets of consecutive layers to prune local redundancy. Retains ~89% of performance after dropping 25% of layers, using just 1% of FineVision for a single epoch.

2026 IEEE SMC Submission
IRIS: Intent Resolution via Inference-time Saccades for Open-Ended VQA in Large Vision-Language Models

Parsa Madinei, Srijita Karmakar, Russell A. Cohen Hoffing, Felix Gervitz, Miguel P. Eckstein

A training-free method that conditions VLM representations on human gaze around speech onset to resolve ambiguous questions — more than doubling accuracy on ambiguous VQA (35% → 77%) with no change to model parameters. Introduces a new real-time gaze + speech + image VQA paradigm and dataset.

2026 NeurIPS Submission
Revealing the Gap in Human and VLM Scene Perception through Counterfactual Semantic Saliency

Ziqi Wen, Parsa Madinei, Miguel P. Eckstein

A black-box, model-agnostic framework (CSS) that scores object importance by the semantic shift caused when an object is causally ablated from a scene. Benchmarking 19 VLMs against a 16,289-response human baseline reveals a pervasive perception gap — models over-rely on large, central, high-saliency objects and under-weight people — with size bias as the primary driver of the divergence.

Activation-Informed Pareto-Guided Low-Rank Compression for Efficient LLM/VLM

Ryan Solgi, Parsa Madinei, Jiayi Tian, Rupak Swaminathan, Jing Liu, Nathan Susanj, Zheng Zhang

PGSVD — a zero-shot compression pipeline that bounds loss change via activation-based errors and casts low-rank compression as bi-objective optimization, yielding Pareto-optimal heterogeneous ranks with better accuracy and inference speedup.
